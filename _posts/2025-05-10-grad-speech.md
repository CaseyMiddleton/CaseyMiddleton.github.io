I know the many graduates and their loved ones sitting here today come from diverse walks of life. And yet, I think we all share the same thought when we look around our modern world:

*"Everything's computer!"*

Computers are everywhere—and for my fellow computer science graduates, that means the demand for our skills only continues to grow. But despite that demand, we graduate at a uniquely challenging time.

Those earning undergraduate degrees are entering a competitive job market shaped by economic uncertainty and the rapid rise of AI. Graduate degree holders face similar pressures, along with deep cuts to the federal research funding that supports the kind of scientific discovery we train for. And many of our international classmates—whose talents brought them to the US for education—now face new barriers to staying here.

The road ahead is sadly not the one we imagined when we began these degrees.

But here's the thing about computer science: it teaches more than just how to write good code. At its core, computer science is about how to break down hard problems, think creatively, and build solutions from scratch. That mindset—the computer science mindset—is what will carry us forward.

I believe in those skills. I believe they'll help us stand out in the job market. I believe they'll help us reimagine the field of computing. One day, I believe those skills will even be the reason we are hired en masse to clean up the tech debt accumulated by overconfident LLMs.

And when it comes down to it, if we can navigate the engineering center, we can navigate these challenging times. So as you find your place in the world, use your influence to pave the way for those who will graduate after you. Let your legacy be one of opening doors, expanding opportunities, and making it easier for the next generation to rise.

Congratulations, class of 2025.
